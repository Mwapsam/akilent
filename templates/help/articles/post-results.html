A WhatsApp chat link on a post shows you which posts get customers talking to you, and what those chats turn into: leads, orders and paid orders.

Before you start

Connect a WhatsApp number from Channels. The chat link opens a chat with that number.

Add a chat link to a post

  1. Open Content Studio and create or edit a post.
  2. Under Platforms → WhatsApp chat link, tick the channels you want a link for.
    • Facebook: the link is added to the end of the caption, so people can tap it.
    • Instagram: links in captions can't be tapped. Copy the link from the post page and put it in your bio or on a story link sticker.
  3. Save, schedule or publish the post as usual.

Each channel gets its own short link, so you can see Facebook and Instagram separately.

What the customer sees

Tapping the link opens WhatsApp with a message ready to send, for example "Hi! I saw your post and I'd like to know more. (ref K7Q2MX)". The code at the end is how the chat is matched to the post. If they delete it before sending, the chat still reaches your inbox, but it isn't counted for the post.

See the results

Open the post in Content Studio. The Results card shows, for each channel:

  • Link clicks: times people opened the link. Link previews and bots aren't counted.
  • Chats: conversations that started from the link. On Instagram this also counts DMs opened by an automatic reply to a comment on the post.
  • Leads and Orders created from those chats, and the total of the orders that were Paid.

Leads and orders that came from a post show "Came from a post" with a link back to it.

What isn't counted

Only chats with clear evidence are counted: the link's code, or a reply to a comment on the post. A customer who saw the post and then messaged you another way isn't counted, so your real results can be higher than the card shows. Akilent never guesses which post someone saw.

Post performance

The Performance card on the same page shows reach, views, likes, comments, saves and shares as Facebook and Instagram report them. The first numbers arrive about an hour after publishing and are updated over the next four weeks. A dash (—) means the number isn't available yet or isn't reported for that kind of post. Hover over it to see why.
